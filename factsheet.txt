# Factsheet: John Pritchard Williamson (1922–2014)

*Numbers in brackets refer to the Sources list in the biography page (biography.txt).*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | John Pritchard Williamson (also John P. Williamson) |
| **Born** | Feb. 22, 1922, Cleveland, Ohio [2][6][7][8] |
| **Died** | Oct. 26, 2014, Parkcliffe Community, Northwood, near Toledo, Ohio; age 92 [1][2][9] |
| **Parents** | John and Jane (Pritchard) Williamson [2][6][10] |
| **Spouse** | Helen (Morgan) Williamson; married Aug. 3, 1945; she died Aug. 5, 2019 [5][10][36] |
| **Children** | Three sons [2][5] |
| **Education** | Kent State University, business administration degree, 1945 [5][12]; business manager of the Kent Stater (1944; again 1945) [30][13]; associate editor, Chestnut Burr [31][29]; Blue Key and Men’s Union president [13] |
| **Profession** | Certified public accountant; Arthur Andersen & Company [2][11][6]; The Toledo Edison Company, 1951–1987 [5][11] |
| **Toledo Edison career** | 1951 Director of Systems and Procedures [2][11][10]; 1962 secretary and treasurer [2][15]; c. 1965 vice president of finance [2][16][10]; 1968 senior vice president [2][10][7]; 1972 president and CEO (youngest since 1921) [1][19][20][21][17]; 1979 chairman and CEO [2][10][18]; 1987 chairman emeritus of Toledo Edison and Centerior Energy [1][5] |
| **Notable projects** | Davis-Besse Nuclear Power Station (at the helm through its 1977 startup) [1][22]; proposed the 1985 affiliation with Cleveland Electric Illuminating that created Centerior Energy [4][23][25] |
| **Industry roles** | Chairman, East Central Area Reliability Council [26][10]; president, Ohio Electric Utility Institute [10]; Edison Electric Institute executive committee; chairman, National Electric Security Committee [10]; chairman, North American Electric Reliability Council (3-year term from 1984) [10][27]; all per his obituary [2] |
| **Civic leadership** | Chairman, Greater Toledo Area United Way (1971); president, Toledo Community Chest (1972) [10][6]; past president, Toledo Kiwanis Club and Toledo Area Chamber of Commerce; past chairman, Ohio Chamber of Commerce [2]; first chairman, Toledo Development Committee [2][6]; Toledo Symphony trustee for nearly three decades, served as chairman [3]; boards include Toledo Museum of Art [28], Riverside Hospital, Toledo Hospital, Toledo Trust, First National Bank of Toledo [24], Red Cross, Junior Achievement, Boys Club [2]; Salvation Army board; a City of Toledo board; Ohio Development Advisory Council (1977) |
| **Honors** | Kent State Distinguished Alumnus Award, 1974 [12][5][2]; Toledo Area Citizen of the Year [2][14]; Kent State University medallion, 1992 [14]; Kentucky Colonel |
| **Kent State legacy** | First life members of the Alumni Association (1975); lead gift for the Williamson Alumni Center (former Curtiss House, the president’s home), dedicated Oct. 10, 1992 [33][35][5] |
| **Retirement** | Homes in Rio Verde, Arizona, and Perrysburg, Ohio; horseback riding; world travel; Stephen Minister at Rio Verde Community Church (1996) |
| **Survivors named in obituary** | Wife Helen (d. 2019); three sons; a grandson and a great-grandson; other family members [2] |
